Industry context & projects

The headline facts in the industry-context note, and the real projects shown as examples of each power architecture.

  • Cleanview, Bypassing the Grid: How Data Center Developers Are Building Their Own Power Plants (2026) — 59 behind-the-meter data centers planning ~90 GW of self-generation (over 25% of planned US capacity); ~2 GW operating in mid-2026, mostly xAI's Colossus gas turbines.
  • IEA, Key Questions on Energy and AI: executive summary (2026-04-16) — 15–27 GW of onsite gas may power data centres by 2030; dependable onsite gas needs 30–70% overbuild relative to demand, used as the redundancy guidance for sizing gas units.
  • EPRI, Powering Intelligence 2026 (Feb 2026) — data centers at 9–17% of US electricity by 2030, up from ~4–5% today (not opened; search index).
  • LBNL, United States Data Center Energy Usage Report: 2025 Update (published Jun 2026) — reference case of 649 TWh in 2030 (11.8% of US demand), bounds 521–843 TWh (not opened; search index). The full report is listed under Cooling & water.
  • Ember, Data centres for grids: the potential impact of flexibility (2026-10-29) — only ~3% of data centre capacity available for load shifting in 2030, since ~87% runs latency-sensitive work; onsite generation offers more grid relief.
  • Fortune: Meta Hyperion and ten Entergy gas plants (2026-03-27) — grid-connected example: three Entergy plants (~2.3 GW) approved Aug 2025 and seven more (~5.2 GW) proposed, ~7.5 GW in total.
  • Intersect: Google Meitner Energy Center, Gray & Roberts Counties, TX (2026-06-04) — hybrid example: data center co-located with over 1 GW of wind, solar and batteries, with on-site gas firming a minority of demand.
  • POWER: Google launches a 1 GW-plus co-located data center and generation complex (2026-06) — hybrid example: Intersect Quantum I and II, 640 MW of solar and 1.3 GWh of batteries beside a Google data center.
  • Utility Dive: Enbridge Cowboy solar + storage for Meta, Cheyenne, WY (2026-05) — hybrid example: 365 MW of solar plus 200 MW / 1,600 MWh of batteries ($1.2B, in service by end-2027) under a large-power utility tariff.
  • TechCrunch: xAI gets permits for 15 natural gas generators in Memphis (2025-07-03) — hybrid example and the mobile bridge-turbine type: 15 Solar SMT-130 units "up to 247 megawatts" (~16.5 MW each).
  • Pacifico Energy: 7.65 GW generation permit for GW Ranch, Pecos County, TX (2026-01-26) — islanded example: air permit for 35 gas turbines plus 1.8 GW of batteries and 750 MWac of solar.
  • DCD: Ohio approves a 200 MW gas plant for Meta in New Albany (2025-06) — islanded example (Williams Socrates, two 200 MW off-grid plants); also why New Albany is a site preset.
  • DCD: Oracle to deploy up to 2.45 GW of Bloom fuel cells in New Mexico (2026-04-27) — islanded example: Project Jupiter's fuel-cell microgrid in place of gas turbines and diesel.

Cooling & water

The hourly PUE and water curves are judgment calibrated to these fleet benchmarks, at a temperate site (Chicago) and a hot desert site (Phoenix).

  • The Green Grid, White Paper #35: Water Usage Effectiveness — the WUE definition used: annual site water withdrawn per kWh of IT energy (L/kWh).
  • Uptime Institute Global Data Center Survey 2026: the growing PUE advantage of larger data centers — industry average PUE 1.52, capacity-weighted 1.36, newer large sites at 1.3 or below; a calibration target for air cooling.
  • Uptime Institute Global Data Center Survey 2025 — rack densities rising mainly in the 10–30 kW range; basis of the ~20 kW air-cooling density limit.
  • LBNL, United States Data Center Energy Usage Report: 2025 Update (eScholarship) — national average PUE 1.45 (2024) and 1.145 for AI-serving facilities; AI server utilization (training ~80%, inference ~20–40%) and idle power behind the IT utilization defaults.
  • LBNL, 2024 United States Data Center Energy Usage Report (Dec 2024) — average site WUE ~0.36 L/kWh, 0.1–0.3 for hyperscale adiabatic systems; UPS efficiency by facility type (Table 4.3) behind the non-cooling overhead.
  • Google data center efficiency — fleet trailing-twelve-month PUE 1.09 and campus values (Eemshaven 1.07, Midlothian 1.11, air-cooled Mesa 1.22–1.28); calibration targets for liquid and evaporative cooling.
  • Google 2025 Environmental Report (2024 data) — data centers withdrew 9,866 and consumed 7,787 million gallons (79% consumed); calibrates cooling-tower water use (~1.3 L withdrawn per IT kWh) and the evaporated/blowdown split.
  • Microsoft: next-generation datacenters consume zero water for cooling (2024-12-09) — fleet WUE 0.30 L/kWh and closed-loop chip-level cooling with near-zero WUE; basis of the zero-water default.
  • Equinix 2025 annual report (10-K) — 2024 annual average PUE 1.39 for a mostly air-cooled colocation fleet; air-chiller calibration target.
  • Data Centers Look to Find Balance Between Demand and Delivery (BNP Media) — Meta's evaporative fleet at PUE 1.09 and WUE 0.26 L/kWh (2021).
  • Schneider Electric White Paper 282: capital cost of liquid vs air cooling (press summary) — immersion equals air capex at 10 kW/rack and is 10–14% cheaper at 20–40 kW/rack; sets the relative cooling capex.
  • ASHRAE TC 9.9 Thermal Guidelines for Data Processing Environments, 5th ed. (2021): reference card — recommended inlet 18–27 °C, allowable A1 15–32 °C and A2 10–35 °C.
  • Upsite: ASHRAE fifth edition liquid-cooling chapter updates — facility-water classes W17 to W45 and W+.
  • Vertiv and NVIDIA GB200 NVL72 power and cooling blueprint (2024) — up to 132 kW per liquid-cooled rack.
  • Facilities Dive: no chillers needed for Vera Rubin racks (CES 2026) — 45 °C supply water without chillers and ~2x Grace Blackwell rack power; basis of the 250 kW cold-plate density limit and dry-cooler free-cooling range.
  • Nautilus Data Technologies: CDU articles — vendor claim of 12–18 month lead times for coolant distribution units.
  • DOE FEMP: cooling towers and how to improve water efficiency — towers typically run 2–4 cycles of concentration, 6+ possible; a check on the 79% evaporated share.
  • ACHR News: open-loop cooling towers or closed-circuit coolers — closed-circuit and dry units cost more up front than open towers; sets cooling-tower capex below dry coolers.
  • DCD: GRC updates its immersion system for higher capacity — up to 200 kW per tank with 32 °C warm water; the immersion density limit.
  • GRC: next-generation immersion cooling with PUE 1.03 (2021-05-18) — vendor claim (cooling only); an upper bound for immersion efficiency.
  • The Register: LiquidStack on immersion cooling (2023-04-14) — single-phase immersion PUE "around 1.05 to 1.1"; the immersion calibration target.
  • Data Center Frontier executive roundtable (2023-06) — chillers "pushing 12 to 16 months"; cross-check on the cooling-plant lead time.
  • R. Stull, Wet-Bulb Temperature from Relative Humidity and Air Temperature (2011) — the wet-bulb formula behind the cooling-tower curves; listed under Sites & weather.

Sites & weather
